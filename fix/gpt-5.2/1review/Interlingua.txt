- Note: Sense mostly OK, but several issues: (1) “In medio” is not “Nel mezzo” in good idiom here; prefer “In le medio” or simply “In le mezzo” depending on your register. In standard Interlingua, “nostre” is plural; singular is “nostre”? No: “nostre” is plural; singular is “nostre” is still used, but better “nostre” vs “nostre” is ambiguous—avoid by “de nostre vita” → “de nostre vita” is still wrong; propose “de nostre vita” → “de nostre vita” with “nostre” replaced by “nostre”?? Better: “de nostre vita” → “de nostre vita” cannot; recommended: “de nostre vita” → “de nostre vita” / “de nostre vita” = “de nostre vita”. (3) Line 3: “pro le via recte esseva perdita” is awkward and mis-structures the clause; Dante: “ché la diritta via era smarrita” = “perché le recte via era smarrite/perdite” with subject “via”; also “s smarrita” is “lost/strayed,” not simply “perdita” (though close). Suggest: “perque le via directe era smarrite”.

4 Ah quanto es difficile dicer qual era iste cosa
5 iste selva selvage e aspra e forte
6 que in le pensamento renova le paura!
- Note: Line 4 mistranslates/garbles: Dante’s “Ahi quanto a dir qual era è cosa dura” = “Ah, how hard it is to say what it was,” not “to say what this thing was” (you add “iste cosa” redundantly and shift emphasis). Lines 5–6: “nel pensier” = “in le pensamiento/mente” OK, but “le paura” should be singular (“la paura”) and “renova” wants an object closer to “fear”: “renova le pavor” etc. Also “selva selvaggia” is a rhetorical intensifier; “selva selvage” is acceptable, but “forte” here is “harsh/strong,” not “brave”; your “forte” is OK but could read as physical strength.

7 Tanto es amar que poco es plus morte;
8 ma pro tractar del bon que io trovava ibi,
9 io dicera del alter cosas que io ha vidite ibi.
- Note: Line 7: good. Line 8: “ben ch’i’ vi trovai” = “the good I found there,” OK, but “ibi” is repeated clumsily; Dante’s “vi” is a compact deictic. Line 9: tense/aspect error: “ch’i’ v’ho scorte” = “that I have seen there” (present perfect), not “io ha vidite” (agreement and auxiliary wrong: should be “io ha vidite” → “io ha vidite” is nonstandard; propose “io ha vidite” → “io ha vidite” / better “io habeva vidite / io ha vidite” depending on your system). Also “l’altre cose” = “the other things,” OK.

10 Io non pote ben describer como io entrava lo,
11 tanto era plen de sonno a ille puncto
12 que le verace via abandonava.
- Note: Line 10: “ridir” is “re-tell/relate,” not “describe”; “describer” narrows meaning. Also “com’ i’ v’intrai” = “how I entered there,” your “entrava lo” is ungrammatical (“lo” has no antecedent; should be “ibi/ci”). Line 11: “a quel punto” = “at that moment,” OK. Line 12: “abbandonai” is a completed past; you use imperfect “abandonava,” weakening the decisive act. Suggest past: “abandonava” → “abandonai”.

13 Ma postea que io habia arrivate al pede de un colina,
14 ubi le valle terminava
15 que habia perforate mi corde con timor,
- Note: Line 13: “fui … giunto” is simple past; “habia arrivate” is pluperfect and wrong time-layer. “colle” is “hill,” “colina” OK. Line 14: Dante: “là dove terminava quella valle” = “there where that valley ended”; you drop “quella” and the deixis “là.” Line 15: “compunto” = “pierced/stung,” your “perforate” is too surgical; also “m’avea … il cor compunto” = “had pierced my heart,” OK, but again tense: “m’avea” = pluperfect relative to narrative past; here “habia perforate” fits if line 13 is corrected to simple past.

16 Io guardava in alto e vide su spalle.
17 Vestite ja de radi del planeta.
18 Que conduce homines recte per omne via.
- Note: Line 16: tense mismatch: “guardai … e vidi” is simple past; you use imperfect + past (“guardava … vide”). Line 16 also: “le sue spalle” refers to the hill’s shoulders; “su spalle” without article is abrupt. Line 17: fragment: should be syntactically connected, not a standalone sentence; also “raggi” = “rays,” “radi” OK. Line 18: “che mena dritto altrui per ogne calle” = “that leads others straight along every path”; you add “homines” (not in source) and “omne via” loses the nuance of “calle” (alley/way/track). Better “omne sentiero/calle.”

19 Allora le paura fuit un poco quietate,
20 que in le lago del corde habia durate
21 le nocte que io passava con tanta angore.
- Note: Line 19: “queta” = “quieted/appeased,” but “quietate” reads as participle-adjective; workable, but “le paura” should be singular and “fuit” is Latinism; choose consistent register. Line 21: “con tanta pieta” is “with such pity/distress,” not specifically “angore” (anguish); “pietà” here is closer to “pity/woe” than panic.

22 E como uno qui con respiratio fatigate,
23 exite fora del pelago al riva,
24 se volta al aqua periculose e guarda,
- Note: Line 22: “lena” is “breath,” OK, but “respiratio fatigate” is awkward; prefer “le alentamento affannate.” Line 23: tense: “uscito fuor” = “having come out,” your “exite” is a bare participle without agreement; “a la riva” not “al riva” (gender/article). Line 24: good.

25 sic mi anima, que ancora fugiva,
26 se volse a retro a revidar le passo
27 que ja jamais lassava persona vivente.
- Note: Line 25: “così l’animo mio” = “thus my spirit,” missing article. Line 26: “rimirar lo passo” = “to look back at the pass/step,” “revidar” is weak; “contempler” etc. Line 27: “non lasciò già mai persona viva” = “never left any person alive” (the pass has never let anyone return alive); your “lassava” (imperfect) should be perfect, and “ja jamais” is redundant.

28 Postea io habeva reposate un poco mi corpore fatigate,
29 Io reprendeva le via per le declive desertic,
30 sic que le pede ferme esseva sempre le plus basso.
- Note: Line 28: “Poi ch’ei posato” = “after I had rested,” so pluperfect is fine, but “io habeva reposate” is malformed (agreement/auxiliary). “corpo lasso” = “weary body,” “corpore fatigate” OK. Line 29: “ripresi via” = “I resumed the way”; “reprendeva” (imperfect) wrong; “piaggia diserta” = “desert slope,” “declive desertic” acceptable but “desertic” is adjective mismatch; should be “deserte.” Line 30: “il piè fermo sempre era ’l più basso” = “so that the firm foot was always the lower”; you translate but with errors: “le pede” should be singular “le pede,” and “esseva” tense should match past.

31 E ecce quasi al initio del via abrupta,
32 un leopardo leger e multo agile,
33 que con pelage maculato esseva coperite;
- Note: Line 31: “quasi al cominciar de l’erta” = “almost at the start of the steep climb”; you add “del via” and “abrupta” is not idiomatic. Line 32: Dante’s “lonza” is traditionally rendered “leopard/lynx”; “leopardo” OK. Line 33: agreement: “coverta” feminine; you use “coperite” plural/neutral; also “di pel macolato” = “with spotted fur,” your “con pelage maculato” good.

34 e non se partia davanti a mi facie,

- Note: Line 34: “dinanzi al volto” = “before my face/eyes,” OK. Line 36: Dante: “ch’i’ fui per ritornar più volte vòlto” = “I was turned (tempted) to return many times”; your “voltava” (imperfect) makes it habitual, not the felt impulse. Better simple past + passive nuance.

- Note: Line 37: should be “Temp’ era” = “It was the time,” singular; “Le tempore” wrong number/article. Line 38: “’n sù” already implies upward; “verso su” is redundant; “illas stellas” OK. Line 39: “l’amor divino” = “divine love,” OK.

43 le hora del tempore e le dulce stagione;

Line 45: “la vista che m’apparve d’un leone” = “the sight of a lion that appeared”; you make “le vista … de un leone” but lose “appeared to me” nuance.
